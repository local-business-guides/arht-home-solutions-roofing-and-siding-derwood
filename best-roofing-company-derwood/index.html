## A short contractor checklist
Before choosing a contractor, gather the same basic information from each company. That makes it easier to spot differences in scope and understand what you’re agreeing to.

  • Confirm the company’s licensing information and ask about relevant manufacturer certifications.
  • Request a written description of the planned work, including materials and areas included.
  • Ask who will coordinate the work and how updates will be shared.
  • Check whether the proposal addresses cleanup and what happens if the project scope needs to change.
  • Keep a copy of the proposal and any related paperwork for your records.

A thorough proposal should help you understand what the contractor plans to do, not leave you guessing about key details. If one item is unclear, ask for an explanation before making a decision.

## Questions to settle before work starts
Ask how the company determines whether repair or replacement makes sense for your roof. You can also ask what materials are being considered and what choices you’ll need to make before the crew arrives. A useful answer explains the options without pushing you to decide on the spot.

Find out how the contractor will handle work that touches other parts of the exterior. Roofing can connect with gutters, siding, and windows, so it helps to clarify which areas are part of the proposal and which are not. If you have concerns about more than one part of the home, mention them during the inspection so the scope can be discussed together.

For storm-related work, ask how the company handles the insurance process. ARHT Home Solutions works directly with the adjuster from the first inspection through final payment, helping document the scope covered by the policy. Review the proposed work and claim paperwork carefully, and ask for clarification on any difference you notice.

## Avoid rushed decisions and know when to call
Don’t choose a contractor based only on a brief conversation or a single line in an estimate. Make sure you understand the work, who is responsible for each part, and how to reach the company. Be cautious about agreeing to a scope you haven’t had time to read or asking a contractor to make decisions for you without explaining them.

Call a professional when you’re unsure whether a roof issue needs a repair, when you’re planning a replacement, or when you want a second set of eyes on the exterior.
